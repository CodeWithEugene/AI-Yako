# Code of Conduct

This project follows the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/), version 2.1, and the conduct expectations of the **AI Yako hackathon** community (DoraHacks, Luma, Discord, and the Node NBO venue).

## Our Pledge

We pledge to make participation in this project and the hackathon a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Our Standards

**Expected behavior**

- Be welcoming, respectful, and constructive in code reviews, on Discord, and at the venue.
- Give and accept feedback gracefully. Critique ideas, not people.
- Credit other people's work. The hackathon also requires disclosing reused code.
- Respect that teammates and other hackers may be remote, in different time zones, and at different skill levels.

**Unacceptable behavior**

- Harassment, insults, or derogatory comments of any kind.
- Publishing others' private information without permission.
- Plagiarism or misrepresenting others' work as your own.
- Any conduct that would be inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces (this repo, issues, PRs) and in hackathon spaces when representing this project, including the AI Yako Discord, Node NBO in Gigiri, and demo day.

## Enforcement

- Report incidents to the project team lead (**TBD**, add contact) or directly to the AI Yako organizers via the hackathon Discord or the [DoraHacks hackathon page](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/ai-yako/detail).
- All complaints will be reviewed promptly and fairly, and reporters' confidentiality will be respected.
- Maintainers may remove, edit, or reject contributions that violate this Code, and may temporarily or permanently ban any contributor for behavior they deem inappropriate, threatening, or harmful.
